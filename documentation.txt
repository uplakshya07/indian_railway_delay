Problem Definition: Indian Railways generates massive volumes of operational data daily -- scheduled timetables, station master records, and actual arrival/departure logs. Train delays across extensive rail networks disrupt travel schedules, reduce operational efficiency, and cascade into system-wide congestion. This project aims to analyze historical train delay data to uncover underlying punctuality patterns, pinpoint high-risk stations and bottlenecks, and forecast delays to enable proactive operational and commuter planning.

Project Description: This project focuses on evaluating a comprehensive dataset of passenger train operations across the Indian Railways network over a full year. By analyzing scheduled timetables, station sequences, and recorded delays across multiple service types, the project addresses three primary objectives:
- Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis: Characterizing delay distributions across different times of the year, days of the week, and train categories (e.g., Express, Superfast, Premium services).
- Bottleneck & High-Risk Route Identification: Mapping geographical and structural stress points across railway zones, identifying critical transit stations and sections that consistently trigger or amplify delays.
- Delay Prediction: Forecasting anticipated arrival and departure delays at subsequent stations along a route, transforming raw operational records into actionable foresight.

DATASET DETAILS:
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rxydenxd/indian-railways-delay-dataset

This dataset contains the delay information for about 7,000 trains from Indian railways network for the past one year (2025-02-08 to 2026-02-07), for each day, for each station in their route.

This dataset is scraped entirely from scratch (etrain.info) using Python by the user with username "RXYDENXD" at www.kaggle.com. The goal was to build a comprehensive, multi-table dataset covering the schedules, station metadata, and real-world delay records for nearly the entire Indian Railways passenger network, spanning a full year of operations.

The scraping pipeline used requests, pandas, multi-threaded scraping, which reduced collection time by approximately 90% compared to sequential scraping.

All cleaning scripts were executed via Jupyter notebooks stored in the `analysis/` directory, and the transformed outputs were routed exclusively to the `results/` directory.

1. Dimension Table Cleaning: Missing Station Addresses
------------------------------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Application of a Global Constant for Nominal Attributes.
*   Before: The resolved station dimension (`dim_station_resolved.csv`) contained 94 missing `station_address` values.
*   Resolution Applied: A Pandas script replaced these null fields with the explicit global constant "UNKNOWN".
*   After/Result: 0 remaining null addresses. Referential integrity is strictly maintained for the OLAP warehouse without discarding any dimensional records. The output was saved as `results/dim_station_resolved.csv`.

2. Schedule Fact Cleaning: Missing Arrival & Departure Times
------------------------------------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Logical Imputation (Zero-Halt Rule) for Interval-Scaled Attributes.
*   Before: The `combined_schedule.csv` dataset had 8,673 missing `arrival_time` and 8,673 missing `departure_time` values. A verification script proved these missing states were structural and intentional: missing arrivals occurred exactly and only at origin stations (`station_no = 1`), while missing departures occurred exactly and only at destination stations.
*   Resolution Applied: The Zero-Halt imputation rule was applied using Pandas. Missing origin arrival times were filled with their scheduled departure time, and missing destination departure times were filled with their scheduled arrival time.
*   After/Result: 0 remaining missing schedule times. This resolves the missing data while preserving the continuous numeric sequences required for time-series predictive models. The cleaned dataset was saved as `results/combined_schedule_imputed.csv`.

3. Delay Field Context Resolution (important for handling missing delay values)
---------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Contextual Hypothesis Testing via Out-of-Core Processing.
*   Before: The exact operational definition of the `delay` column in the ~30 million row `combined_delay.csv` dataset was ambiguous (whether it represented arrival delay or departure delay).
    Our defined composite hypothesis posits that the delay field is a context-dependent metric that switches meaning based on the station's position in the route. Specifically, it assumes that:
        - Departure Delay is recorded exclusively at the train's source or origin station.
        - Arrival Delay is recorded at all subsequent intermediate and destination stations.

*   Resolution Applied: A hypothesis was formulated to test delays at route boundaries (origin vs. destination). A DuckDB SQL query, explicitly filtering out null values, evaluated all 38 million records and yielded 1,038,047 non-zero delays at origin stations and 1,044,006 non-zero delays at destination stations.
*   After/Result: The composite context switch hypothesis was confirmed. The `delay` field is context-dependent: it strictly records Departure Delay at the source station (`station_no = 1`) and Arrival Delay at all intermediate and destination stations (`station_no > 1`). These findings establish the foundational ETL rules for numeric modeling and were successfully exported to `results/step02_01_ETL_FINDINGS.json`.

ETL / DATA CLEANING (STEP 2.2) — SPATIOTEMPORAL IMPUTATION OF MISSING DELAYS

Following the resolution of the delay field context, the ETL pipeline addressed the 1,874,271 structured missing values in the 38-million-row combined_delay.csv fact table. Because train delays cascade sequentially across a network, missing values could not be blindly replaced with zero. A three-step out-of-core DuckDB script was executed to resolve these gaps while preserving time-series continuity for future predictive models.

1. Phantom-Journey Drop (Multi-Day Aware)
*   Initial doubt: Certain train journeys might have logged zero valid delays across their entire route, representing completely missing sequences (cancelled or short-terminated trains).
*   Technique Applied: Multi-day aware group-wise filtering and conditional deletion.
*   Resolution: Journeys with 0 valid delays were identified and entirely dropped before any imputation occurred. Crucially, journeys were grouped by `origin_date` (calculated as `date - (arrival_day - 1)`) rather than just `date` to ensure multi-day train runs were treated as single continuous entities. This prevented the pipeline from generating "phantom trains" that would artificially skew bottleneck identification.

2. Origin Imputation (Per-Train Median & Global Fallback)
*   Initial Discovery: Forward-filling cannot apply to origin stations (`station_no = 1`) because there is no preceding station to pull data from.
*   Technique Applied: Class-specific central tendency with a global fallback.
*   Resolution: Missing departure delays at origin stations were replaced with the median departure delay calculated exclusively for that specific `train_no`. If a specific train had absolutely no historical origin data to compute a median, the system fell back to using the global dataset median for origins. This established a mathematically sound baseline for the start of the journey. 

3. Spatiotemporal Imputation (Forward-Filling)
*   Initial Discovery: Intermediate and destination stations contained mid-route reporting gaps due to tracking blackouts.
*   Technique Applied: Spatiotemporal Interpolation (Last Observation Carried Forward).
*   Resolution: A time-series window function (`LAST_VALUE ... IGNORE NULLS`) was used to drag the preceding known physical delay forward to fill the intermediate gaps. This window was partitioned by `origin_date` and `train_no` (not `date` and `train_no`) to ensure the forward-fill correctly crossed midnight boundaries for multi-day journeys. 

4. Orphaned Null Drop & Provenance Tracking
*   Initial Discovery: Some mid-route gaps might occur before the first valid station observation, leaving "orphaned" nulls that forward-filling cannot resolve.
*   Technique Applied: Provenance flagging and targeted deletion.
*   Resolution: An `is_imputed` boolean column was introduced throughout the ETL to track whether a delay was observed (`FALSE`) or filled by the pipeline (`TRUE`). Any unresolved orphaned nulls remaining after Step 3 (10,990 rows) were dropped to ensure 100% data completeness for downstream modeling.

5. Verification and Export
*   Technique Applied: Statistical auditing using Pandas and DuckDB.
*   Result: The datasets successfully verified at 0 remaining missing values. The fully imputed 36.6 million row fact table (exactly 36,629,806 rows) was safely exported without modifying the raw source data.

Conclusion and Next Stage
Step 2.2 is officially complete. The structurally missing delay values have been fully resolved without injecting phantom congestion into the dataset, and all synthetic values are explicitly trackable via the `is_imputed` flag.

ETL / DATA CLEANING (STEP 2.3) — NOISE REDUCTION AND OUTLIER BOUNDARY DEFINITION

Following the spatiotemporal imputation of missing delays, the final step of the ETL pipeline focused on smoothing noisy continuous variables and defining the mathematical boundaries for extreme real-world outliers (bottlenecks) in the 36.6-million-row imputed delay dataset (36,629,806 rows: 35,596,900 observed and 1,032,906 imputed).

Defining Noise vs. Outliers in Train Delays:
- Noise: Random errors or variance (e.g., faulty instruments, data entry problems, transmission errors). For example, a typographical error logging a 525,592-minute (365-day) delay is garbage data that must be removed before outlier detection to prevent it from blurring the distinction between normal objects and true anomalies.
- Outliers: A data object that deviates significantly from the rest of the dataset, appearing as if it were generated by a different mechanism.
- The Project Context: In anomaly mining, "one person’s garbage could be another person’s treasure". While a 14-hour delay deviates wildly from the mean, it is an exceptional treasure indicating a severe structural bottleneck rather than a random error. Our goal is to preserve these genuine extremes while removing impossible transmission errors.

1. Negative Delay (Early Arrival) Validation & Punctuality
- Initial Discovery: A DuckDB query identified 609,727 negative delay records (1.6646% of the dataset; 584,981 observed and 24,746 imputed), with a mean of -13.10 minutes, median of -10.0 minutes, and standard deviation of 11.09 minutes.
- Domain Verification: Since the dataset was scraped from an Indian Railways tracking platform (etrain.info, sourced from the National Train Enquiry System), negative values intentionally log early arrivals. The maximum early arrival is -120.0 minutes (2 hours), which is physically valid due to schedule recovery buffers.
- Result: All 609,727 negative values were retained intact.
- Punctuality vs. Anomaly: While early arrivals are not the target of our outlier hunt, they are a primary concern for Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis and will be grouped into an "Early / On-Time" category in the OLAP cubes.

2. Noise Removal (Domain Knowledge Cutoff)
- Initial Discovery: Prior to filtering, the dataset exhibited a maximum delay of 525,592.0 minutes (~365 days), inflating the global standard deviation to 229.88 minutes (mean: 36.75 minutes).
- Resolution Applied: In Indian Railways operations, a 24-hour (1,440-minute) delay is the standard operational cutoff beyond which daily trains are rescheduled or cancelled to avoid conflicting with the next day's run.
- Result: A hard cutoff at 1,440 minutes removed 14,982 noise records (14,582 observed and 400 imputed). The cleaned table (combined_delay_cleaned.csv) retains 36,614,824 rows (35,582,318 observed and 1,032,506 imputed, or 2.8199% imputed) with 0 nulls and preserves the `is_imputed` provenance flag.

3. Outlier Boundary Definition (Statistical Dispersion)
- The Physics of Railway Speed (The Left Wall): Train delays are heavily right-skewed. Because the physical minimum hits a hard wall at -120.0 minutes (close to the 2 SD left boundary of -96.45 minutes), standard deviation is applied exclusively on the unbounded positive side to isolate bottlenecks.
- Result: Global and positive-tail statistics were recalculated on the cleaned dataset:
  * All Cleaned Records (<= 1,440 mins): True Mean = 35.60 minutes, True SD = 66.03 minutes (Observed-only check: Mean = 35.68, SD = 65.85).
    - High-Risk Zone (> 2 SD = 167.65 minutes): 1,273,458 records.
    - Critical Zone (> 3 SD = 233.68 minutes): 659,943 records.
  * Positive Tail Only (0 < delay <= 1,440 mins): True Mean = 42.25 minutes, True SD = 69.62 minutes (Observed-only check: Mean = 42.27, SD = 69.38).
    - High-Risk Zone (> 2 SD = 181.48 minutes): 1,094,178 records.
    - Critical Zone (> 3 SD = 251.09 minutes): 563,763 records.

4. Concept Hierarchy Generation (Time Dimension)
- Resolution Applied: Extracted cyclic temporal attributes (year, quarter, month, month_name, day_of_month, day_name, day_of_week, is_weekend) across all 365 contiguous calendar dates (2025-02-08 to 2026-02-07) into `results/dim_date.csv`.

DATA VALIDATION (STEP 2.5) — STOP ALIGNMENT RESOLUTION

Initial Discovery: In Step 2.4, 18.0147% of cleaned delay rows (6,596,040 rows) carried a different station_name than combined_schedule_imputed.csv at the same station_no. Because stop alignment depends only on (train_no, station_no, station_name) and not on date, the 36,614,824 cleaned delay rows were collapsed into 141,646 distinct stops (weighted by row count) in DuckDB (analysis/step02_05_alignment_diagnosis.ipynb). Station names were verified to be 100% stable per (train_no, station_no) across all dates (0 unstable stops).

1. Classification of Every Delay Stop
Each delay stop was compared with the schedule at the same station_no and, if different, searched by station_name within that train's schedule:
- exact: 81.9853% of rows (30,018,784 rows: 29,661,304 observed, 357,480 imputed; 112,948 stops / 79.7396% of stops).
- shifted: 15.6698% of rows (5,737,461 rows: 5,618,694 observed, 118,767 imputed; 24,495 stops / 17.2931% of stops). The station exists in that train's schedule at a different station_no.
- different_station: 2.3449% of rows (858,579 rows: 302,320 observed, 556,259 imputed; 4,203 stops / 2.9673% of stops). The station_no exists in the schedule, but the recorded delay station_name does not appear in that train's schedule.
- orphan_name_not_in_schedule & train_not_in_schedule: 0 stops in combined_delay_cleaned.csv (unscheduled stop numbers beyond the schedule route length were already removed during Step 2.2).
- format_only (spelling, whitespace, or case differences): 0 stops. Every mismatch is a genuine stop-sequence difference rather than a formatting issue.

2. Root Cause
The shifts (sched_station_no - delay_station_no) are predominantly negative:
- -1: 3,596,911 rows (15,944 stops; 62.6917% of shifted rows)
- -2: 1,073,818 rows (4,402 stops; 18.7159% of shifted rows)
- -3: 366,257 rows (1,413 stops; 6.3836% of shifted rows)
- -4: 168,093 rows (622 stops; 2.9297% of shifted rows)
- +1: 145,450 rows (626 stops; 2.5351% of shifted rows)
- -5: 99,158 rows (400 stops; 1.7283% of shifted rows)
The delay table records extra intermediate stops not listed in the static schedule, pushing subsequent delay station_no values forward along the route. This matches the raw route-length comparison across all 7,033 raw delay trains: 4,902 trains have identical route lengths in the schedule, whereas 1,918 trains are 1 to 8 stops longer in the raw delay table, 95 are >8 stops longer, 85 are shorter, and 33 are absent from the schedule. Across the 6,999 trains in the cleaned delay dataset, 4,964 trains are fully clean, 833 are under half affected, 1,019 are over half affected, and 183 are fully affected.

3. Resolution Applied
- Technique Applied: Built and validated `stop_alignment_map.csv` (train_no, delay_station_no, sched_station_no, cls). Exact stops retain their station_no; shifted stops are re-pointed to the matching station_name within the same train's schedule.
- Result: 35,756,245 rows (97.6551%) across 137,443 stops (97.0327%) were cleanly aligned with the schedule, with 0 ambiguous name matches, 0 collisions, and 0 duplicate target (train_no, sched_station_no) keys. The remaining 858,579 rows (2.3449%, 4,203 stops; 302,320 observed and 556,259 imputed) are retained in the fact table with `sched_station_no = NULL` so operational delay records are preserved without attaching mismatched schedule attributes.

Conclusion: Schedule features must be joined exclusively through `stop_alignment_map.csv` on (train_no, delay_station_no -> sched_station_no), never directly on raw station_no. Physical station identity always comes from the delay record's `station_name`.
